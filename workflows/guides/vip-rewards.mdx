| **Goal** | Increase the tenure of the subscribers who bring the most revenue |
| **Primary metric** | Retention of the top-value cohort, for example the top 10–20% of subscribers by revenue |
| **Supporting metrics** | Share of subscribers reaching each milestone, reward cost as a share of cohort revenue |
| **Triggers** | Next order updated (`upcoming_order_generated`), Subscription anniversary (`subscription_anniversary`) |
| **Audience** | Long-tenure or high-revenue subscribers |
| **Default outcome** | Next order updated: the order is approved unless a rule blocks it |

## Why it works

Revenue in a subscription business is concentrated. A minority of subscribers, those with larger orders or longer tenure, usually accounts for a large share of recurring revenue. Losing one of them costs as much as losing several average subscribers.

These subscribers rarely need a discount to stay. What keeps them is recognition and a reason to reach the next point: a gift on a known milestone, a perk that comes with a status, a thank-you on their anniversary. Predictable milestones work better than random surprises, because a subscriber who knows a reward is two orders away has a reason not to cancel before then.

## Flow

The rules run in your store's **Next order updated** workflow, which shapes every upcoming renewal order. If the store already has one, add these steps as branches of it.

1. **Milestone gift.** When the upcoming order is the subscriber's 6th (or 12th, 24th…), add a free product to it.
2. **VIP status.** When a customer's total revenue passes your threshold, tag them as VIP and apply a VIP perk to their orders, such as a small discount or free shipping.
3. **Anniversary.** On the subscription's anniversary, send an event to your email platform for a thank-you message that mentions their milestone and perks.

## Configuration choices

- **Milestones.** Pick points just beyond where your retention curve drops, so the reward lands where subscribers would otherwise leave. Check your [cohort retention](/analytics) report.
- **Gift choice.** A product the subscriber does not already receive works best: it costs you product cost, not full price, and introduces another item they may add later.
- **VIP threshold.** Base it on customer revenue or subscription tenure (`subscription.cycle_count`). Set it so the VIP group stays small enough to feel exclusive.
- **Perk type.** Free shipping and early access cost less than a permanent discount and are easier to explain.

## Guardrails

- **One Next order updated workflow.** Add VIP logic to the existing workflow rather than replacing it, or other order rules stop applying.
- **Check the milestone in a test run.** Confirm on a test subscription that the gift lands on the intended order before publishing.
- **Keep rewards simple.** One clear perk is easier to understand and communicate than several small ones.
- **Make VIP visible to your team.** The tag lets support and marketing recognise VIP customers in Shopify and your email platform.

## Measure results

- **Retention of the VIP and milestone cohorts** before and after launch.
- **Share of subscribers passing each milestone.** It should rise around the rewarded orders.
- **Reward cost** as a share of the cohort's revenue. If cost grows faster than retained revenue, raise the threshold or change the perk.
