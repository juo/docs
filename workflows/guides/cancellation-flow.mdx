|---|---|
| **Goal** | Understand cancellation reasons and save subscriptions that can continue on better-fitting terms |
| **Primary metric** | Save rate: share of cancellation attempts that end with the subscription kept |
| **Supporting metrics** | Cancellation reasons, retention of saved subscriptions 2–3 cycles later, discount cost per save |
| **Trigger** | Subscription cancellation requested (`cancellation_clicked`) |
| **Audience** | Every subscriber who clicks **Cancel** in the Customer Portal |
| **Default outcome** | The subscription is cancelled unless the subscriber accepts an offer |

## Why it works

Many cancellations are not a rejection of the product. Subscribers leave because they have too much stock, the price feels high this month, the timing is wrong, or they never built the habit. Each of these has a better answer than cancelling: a delay, a different frequency, a different product, or a one-off incentive.

A cancellation flow matches the answer to the reason. It also records the reason for every attempt, which shows where the product, pricing, or delivery cadence is losing subscribers.

The revenue effect is direct. Each saved subscription adds at least one more paid cycle, and subscribers who move to a frequency or product that fits them tend to stay longer than those who are kept with a discount alone.

## Flow

![Cancellation flow example](https://cdn.juo.io/content-uploads/cancellation_flow_1_e2373e3403.png)

1. **Remind them of the value.** Show content about what the subscription gives them: savings, convenience, results. Keep it short.
2. **Ask for the reason.** A poll with four to six options. Each option becomes a branch.
3. **Offer the alternative that fits the reason:**
   - *Too much product* → offer to delay the next order, then to change frequency.
   - *Too expensive* → offer a limited discount if they have not had one before, otherwise offer a pause.
   - *Not seeing results / not using it* → show usage guidance, then offer a product swap.
   - *Other* → collect open feedback.
4. **Confirm the outcome.** A confirmation for saved subscriptions. If every offer is declined, the subscription is cancelled.

## Configuration choices

- **Reasons.** Use the reasons your support team hears most. Keep the list short; long polls lower completion. Review the "Other" feedback monthly and promote recurring answers to their own option.
- **Offer size.** Start with a modest discount limited to one or two cycles. A permanent discount saves the subscription but lowers the value of every future order.
- **New vs. long-term subscribers.** Branch on `subscription.cycle_count` or `subscription.age_days` before the poll. New subscribers usually need guidance on using the product; long-term subscribers more often need a cadence change.
- **Product-specific routes.** Branch on `subscription.items contains_where variantId equals …` to give a flagship or newly launched product its own content and feedback questions.

## Guardrails

- **One discount per customer.** Gate discount offers with a usage limit so subscribers cannot cancel repeatedly to collect them.
- **Don't stack offers on one reason.** Two offers in a row is the practical maximum. More feels like an obstacle and drives complaints and chargebacks.
- **Always allow cancellation.** Every path must end in a way out. Hiding it hurts trust and may conflict with consumer protection rules in your markets.

## Measure results

- **Save rate**, overall and per reason. A reason with a low save rate needs a different offer.
- **Retention of saved subscriptions** two to three cycles later. If most saved subscriptions cancel at the next renewal, the offer is delaying churn, not preventing it.
- **Cost per save**: discount value and free products given, compared with the revenue of the extra cycles.
- Compare offers with an [experiment](/workflows/experiments) before rolling one out to all subscribers.
