| **Goal** | Restart canceled and paused subscriptions, with an always-on email and with occasional campaigns |
| **Primary metric** | Reactivation rate: the share of emailed subscribers who restart within 30 days |
| **Supporting metrics** | Revenue per restarted subscription, share that cancel again within two orders, offer cost |

## Why it matters

A cancellation is a stage in the customer journey, not the end of it. Canceled and paused subscribers already know your product, and their subscription, address and payment method are still in Juo. Bringing them back takes one email and a good reason, with no acquisition cost.

Many subscribers leave for reasons that pass: too much stock, a trip, a tight month. An email that reaches them when the need is back, with a restart that takes one click, recovers revenue you would otherwise write off.

## How it works

1. **The email.** The subscriber receives your offer, a reminder of the products they used to get, and a button to restart.
2. **The restart.** One click opens your Customer Portal, signs them in and restarts the subscription. No password and no checkout.
3. **The welcome back.** A message thanks them for coming back and shows their offer. One more click adds the discount or gift to their next orders.

Behind the scenes, the button link carries a tag: `utm_content=juo_reactivate` for canceled subscriptions, `utm_content=juo_unpause` for paused ones. The **Reactivation campaign** workflow recognizes the tag and shows the message. Subscribers who restart any other way don't see it and don't get the offer.

This guide uses Klaviyo. [Customer.io](/quick-actions-cio) receives the same one-click links and custom events, so the same setup works there, and any channel that can send the link with its tag can lead to the same welcome back.

You can send the email in two ways. Both use the same workflow and the same email template.

|  | Automated | One-time campaign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sends** | A set time after a subscriber cancels or pauses | When you run it |
| **Reaches** | Every subscriber who cancels or pauses | The subscriptions you select in Juo |
| **Starts from** | Juo's _Canceled Subscription_ or _Paused Subscription_ event | A custom event sent by a Juo workflow |
| **Best for** | An always-on win-back | Black Friday, a product launch, a new season, a price change |

Most brands run both. The automated email catches every cancellation while it's recent, and a campaign a few times a year reaches everyone who left earlier.

## Plan the campaign

- **The offer.** A discount on the next few orders or a gift with the next one. Keep it to a limited number of orders, so you pay for the return, not for every future order.
- **The timing.** For canceled subscriptions, 30 to 60 days after the cancellation. Subscribers who only needed a short break would have skipped or paused, so give them time to miss the product. Choose the shorter end for products used up quickly, the longer end for slow ones, and avoid landing on top of your other campaigns. For paused subscriptions, a few weeks into the pause. To try again later, for example after 90 days or a year, send a one-time campaign.
- **The message.** Lead with the offer, show the products they subscribed to, and give one reason to return that isn't price: a new flavor, an improved formula, a product launch.
- **Who to leave out.** Subscribers who canceled over a complaint until support has resolved it, and customers with an open chargeback. Use the _CancellationReason_ property of the event to tailor or exclude messages.

## Set it up

Before you start, check that:

- **The Klaviyo integration is enabled.** Juo sends subscription events, product details and one-click links to Klaviyo only while it's on, and events from before you enable it aren't synced. See [Enable the Klaviyo integration](/enable-klaviyo-integration).
- **Your Customer Portal is live.** The email button opens it, and the welcome-back message appears there. See [Customer Portal](/customer-portal).
- **You have a test subscription** with your own email address, to try the whole path before subscribers do.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set how long email links work">
    Every one-click link in a Juo email signs the subscriber in, and it stops working after the **Authentication token expiry**, which is **3 days** by default.

    The link is created when Juo sends the event to Klaviyo, not when Klaviyo sends the email. A flow that waits 14 days after a cancellation sends a link that expired 11 days earlier.

    1. In Juo, go to **Settings → Retention → Quick actions**.
    2. Set **Authentication token expiry** to about 10 days longer than your flow's wait time, so subscribers have time to open the email. For a 60-day wait, set 70 days.

    Anyone who has the email can use its links until they expire, so don't set it longer than your flows need. One-time campaigns create new links when you send them, so they work with any expiry.

    <Warning>
      The expiry applies to every quick link and quick action Juo sends, including your renewal reminders.
    </Warning>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the workflow from the blueprint">
    1. Go to **Workflows → Blueprints** and open **Reactivation campaign**.
    2. Select **Use this blueprint** and follow **Quick setup**.
    3. **Set the offer.** The built-in workflow has one **Apply discount** step for reactivated subscriptions (previously canceled) and one for unpaused subscriptions. Set the type, value and number of orders, or replace a step with **Add product** to give a gift.
    4. **Write the welcome-back message.** Each branch shows a message with the **Reactivation perks** or **Unpause perks** block. Set the same discount and gift in the block as in the workflow step, so the message shows what the subscriber gets.
    5. **Publish** the workflow.

    The workflow starts on the **Customer portal opened** trigger and checks the `utm_content` tag of the link. Only one published workflow can use a trigger. If you already run a workflow on **Customer portal opened**, such as a welcome message, combine both in one workflow. See [Blueprints](/workflows/blueprints#considerations).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the email">
    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Automated">
        Create one flow for canceled subscriptions and one for paused ones.

        **Canceled subscriptions**

        1. In Klaviyo, create a flow triggered by **Canceled Subscription**.
        2. Add a flow filter: **What someone has done → Reactivated Subscription → zero times since starting this flow**. Klaviyo checks flow filters before each email, so subscribers who restart on their own don't receive it.
        3. Add a **time delay**, for example 45 days. Keep it about 10 days shorter than the token expiry from step 1.
        4. Add an email and paste the [email template](#email-template). Its button uses the `Reactivate` link with `utm_content=juo_reactivate`.

        **Paused subscriptions**

        1. Create a flow triggered by **Paused Subscription**.
        2. Add a flow filter: **Properties about someone → Shopify tags contains paused_subscriber**, so subscribers who already unpaused don't receive it.
        3. Add a time delay and an email with the template. Change the button link to `Resume` with `utm_content=juo_unpause`, as shown in [Adapt the template](#adapt-the-template).

        For a short sequence, add a second email a week after the first, behind the same filter.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="One-time campaign">
        You choose who gets the email in Juo, and they receive it within minutes. Behind the scenes, a Juo workflow sends a custom event to Klaviyo for each subscription you select, and a Klaviyo flow sends the email when the event arrives. The event carries the same subscription details as Juo's standard events, with links created at the moment you send.

        **In Juo**

        1. Create a workflow with the **Admin initiated** trigger.
        2. Add the **Send event to Klaviyo** action and enter an event name, for example `Reactivation Campaign`.
        3. Publish the workflow.
        4. Run it once on a test subscription, so Klaviyo creates the metric: go to **Subscriptions**, select the subscription and choose **Run workflow**.

        **In Klaviyo**

        1. Create a flow triggered by the **Reactivation Campaign** metric.
        2. Add a **conditional split**: **Properties about someone → Shopify tags contains paused_subscriber**.
        3. Add an email with the template on each branch: the `Resume` link on the paused branch, the `Reactivate` link on the other.
        4. Set the flow live.

        **Send the campaign**

        1. In Juo, go to **Subscriptions** and filter by **Status: Cancelled** and **Paused**.
        2. Optionally, add more [filters](/managing-subscriptions#find-subscriptions) to target the campaign, such as **Cancelled on** or **Product**.
        2. Select the subscriptions and choose **Run workflow**.

        Klaviyo sends the emails within minutes. To report on each campaign separately, change the event name in the workflow before you send the next one, for example `Black Friday Reactivation`, and create a flow for it.

        <Note>
          Only one published workflow can use the **Admin initiated** trigger. To run other admin workflows, unpublish this one after the send.
        </Note>
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the whole path">
    1. Cancel a test subscription with your own email address, and pause another.
    2. Preview the email in Klaviyo with your profile and check the products and the offer.
    3. Send it to yourself and click the button, as a subscriber would. Check that the subscription restarts, the welcome-back message shows your offer, and after you claim it the discount or gift appears on the next order.

    If the message doesn't appear, check that the link ends with the right `utm_content` value. If you use Klaviyo's automatic UTM tracking, make sure it doesn't set `utm_content`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What's in the template

| Section | What it shows | What to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preview text** | The line shown next to the subject in the inbox | Match it to your offer |
| **Brand** | Your store name from Klaviyo | Replace it with your logo |
| **Headline and message** | A greeting with the subscriber's first name | Your own words and tone |
| **Offer** | The offer, in a highlighted box | Must match the offer in the workflow |
| **Button** | The one-click restart link with its `utm_content` tag | The link and tag for paused subscribers, the button label |
| **Your subscription** | Each product with its image, name, variant and quantity, and the delivery frequency, from the event's `Items` | Usually nothing. Remove the section if you show a single hero product instead |
| **Why subscribers stay** | Three reasons to subscribe | Your real benefits, such as free shipping or member prices |
| **Review link** | A link to the subscription in the Customer Portal | Nothing |
| **Footer** | Your address and an unsubscribe link from Klaviyo | Nothing. Klaviyo requires both in marketing emails |

The colors are in the inline styles: `#1a1a1a` for the button and text, `#f4f3ef` for the background and the offer box. Replace them with your brand colors.

### Adapt the template

**Paused subscribers.** Change the button link to `Resume` and the tag to `juo_unpause`:

```html
<a href="{{ event.QuickActions.Resume }}&utm_source=klaviyo&utm_medium=email&utm_content=juo_unpause" ...>Resume my deliveries</a>
```

Change the headline and message to match, for example "Ready for your next delivery?"

| Purpose | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Seasonal campaign, such as Black Friday | Name the occasion in the headline and preview text. If you give the offer an end date, unpublish the workflow or change its offer when the date passes |
| Product launch | Replace **Why subscribers stay** with the new product: an image, two lines on what's new and a link to its page |
| Product improvement | Use the message to explain what changed, especially for subscribers whose _CancellationReason_ mentions the product |
| Gift instead of a discount | Change the offer box, for example "A free travel bag with your next order" |
| No offer | Remove the offer box and the "claim a thank-you" sentence. Leave the workflow's discount and gift steps out too |

## Guardrails

- **Make the offer worth returning for, not worth leaving for.** If the reactivation offer beats what active subscribers get, some will cancel to collect it.
- **One offer per subscriber.** The welcome-back message has a display limit, so a subscriber who opens the link twice can't claim the offer twice. Keep it when you edit the workflow.
- **Keep sequences short.** One or two emails per cancellation. After that, move the subscriber to your regular marketing.
- **Keep links valid.** When you change a flow's wait time, change the token expiry too.

## How to measure it

- **Reactivation rate.** In Klaviyo, set the flow's conversion metric to **Reactivated Subscription** to see how many recipients restarted. For paused subscribers, compare the number of active subscriptions before and after the send.
- **Offers claimed.** The workflow's runs show how many subscribers saw the welcome-back message and claimed the offer.
- **Repeat cancellations.** The share of restarted subscriptions that cancel again within two orders. A high rate means the offer brings subscribers back for one discounted order only.
- **Return on the offer.** Revenue from restarted subscriptions against the cost of the discounts and gifts. See [Analytics](/analytics).
