| **Goal** | Use seasonal moments to win new subscribers and reward existing ones, without stacking discounts you didn't plan |
| **Primary metric** | New subscriptions during the campaign, and subscriber cancellations during and after it |
| **Supporting metrics** | Average order value, campaign cost per subscriber, retention of campaign subscribers 2–3 orders later |

## Why it matters

A store-wide sale is aimed at new and one-time customers, but your subscribers see it too. Two things can go wrong. If the sale price and the subscription discount combine, subscribers pay much less than you planned. If they don't, subscribers can find the sale price lower than their own, and some cancel to buy at the sale price instead.

A campaign designed with subscribers in mind avoids both. It gives subscribers their own reason to stay, and it turns sale traffic into new subscriptions rather than one-off discounted orders.

## The strategy

### Decide who the campaign is for

- **New customers.** The goal is to start subscriptions. Make the subscription option the best deal in the sale, for example a bigger first-order discount for subscribing.
- **Existing subscribers.** The goal is to keep them and raise order value. Give them something the sale doesn't offer: a gift, an exclusive add-on, or a discount on their next order.
- **Both.** Run two offers with clear rules for each, so the same order can't collect both.

### Choose the incentive

| Incentive | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gift in the next order | Rewarding subscribers, introducing new products | Stock, and gifts landing on the right order |
| Discount on the next order or next few orders | Matching the sale for subscribers | Make it time-limited, not permanent |
| Temporary change to the subscription discount | A seasonal price for every subscriber | Changing it back after the campaign |
| Volume or bundle discount | Raising order value | Stacking with the subscription discount |
| Exclusive add-on at a special price | Raising order value without discounting the subscription | Too many choices at once |

### Plan timing and communication

- **Tell subscribers first.** A message before the public sale, with their own offer, makes them feel looked after instead of left out.
- **Make the offer easy to claim.** One click from the email or a visible offer in the Customer Portal works better than a code to remember.
- **Set an end date** and say it. Open-ended offers lose urgency and are easy to forget to switch off.

## Guardrails

- **Decide on stacking before the sale starts.** Know exactly which discounts can combine for subscriptions, and test an order before launch.
- **Don't make cancelling and rebuying the best deal.** The subscriber offer should be worth at least as much as the sale.
- **Limit gifts and one-off discounts** to one per subscriber, and check you have the stock.
- **Plan the end of the campaign.** Write down what changes back, and when.

## Examples

- **Black Friday.** New customers get the store-wide sale, with the biggest saving reserved for subscribing. Existing subscribers get a free gift in their next order, announced a few days before the public sale.
- **Subscription anniversary.** Subscribers who reach one year get a thank-you gift or a discount on their next order.
- **Seasonal price.** For a holiday month, every subscriber gets a higher subscription discount, which returns to normal after the campaign.

## How to measure it

- **New subscriptions** during the campaign compared with a normal period, and how many renew after the first order.
- **Subscriber cancellations** during the sale and in the weeks after. A spike means the sale undercut your subscribers.
- **Cost per subscriber** of gifts and discounts, compared with the extra orders and order value.
