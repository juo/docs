A playbook is a workflow pattern with the business reasoning behind it: which metric it moves, why it works, how the flow is built, and how to tell whether it is working. Use playbooks as a starting point in the workflow builder or as a brief for an AI agent connected to Juo.

## How workflows move subscription revenue

Subscription revenue comes from a small set of levers:

- **Recurring revenue** = active subscribers × average order value (AOV) × orders per subscriber per period
- **Lifetime value (LTV)** ≈ AOV × gross margin × number of paid cycles before a subscriber leaves
- **Number of paid cycles** grows when fewer subscribers cancel, skip, pause, or fail payment in each cycle

Every playbook targets one of these levers. Small changes compound: a subscriber kept for one more cycle adds a full order of revenue, and a subscriber kept past an early drop-off point is likely to stay for several more.

| Lever | What changes | Example playbooks |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer cancellations | Subscribers who try to leave stay on better-fitting terms | [Cancellation flow](/workflows/guides/cancellation-flow) |
| Fewer early drop-offs | New subscribers reach their second and third orders | [Fight new subscriber churn](/workflows/guides/fight-new-subscriber-churn) |
| Longer high-value tenure | Top subscribers have a reason to stay to the next milestone | [VIP rewards](/workflows/guides/vip-rewards) |
| Reactivated subscribers | Cancelled subscribers return without new acquisition cost | [Win back cancelled subscribers](/workflows/guides/churned-subs-winback) |

## How a playbook is structured

Each playbook follows the same sections so people and agents can find the same information in the same place:

- **At a glance**: goal, primary metric, trigger, and audience.
- **Why it works**: the subscriber behaviour behind the problem and how the flow changes it.
- **Flow**: the steps and branches in plain language.
- **Configuration choices**: the decisions to make for your store, such as offer size or timing.
- **Guardrails**: limits that keep the flow from giving away margin or showing up too often.
- **Measure results**: what to track, and how to tell a real improvement from a shifted problem.

For the building blocks used in each flow, see [Triggers](/workflows/triggers), [Conditions](/workflows/conditions), and [Actions](/workflows/actions).

## Rules that apply to every playbook

- **One published workflow per trigger.** If a playbook uses a trigger your store already uses, add its logic as a branch of the existing workflow instead of creating a second one.
- **Lead with value, then offer.** Content that reminds subscribers why they subscribed costs nothing. Incentives cost margin and train subscribers to ask for them.
- **Limit every incentive.** Use usage limits on offers and display limits on content, so a subscriber cannot collect the same offer repeatedly.
- **Test before you scale.** Use an [experiment](/workflows/experiments) to compare offers or messages on real traffic before you settle on one.
